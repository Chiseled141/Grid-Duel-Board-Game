# Heraldry references for manuscript-v1

Checked online before generating the fronts, 19 October 2026. These are stylized game illustrations, not facsimiles of one surviving armorial. Outer frame colors are game identifiers and do not alter the colors of the shield itself. The user explicitly approved later seventeenth-century heraldry for Transylvania.

| Region | Shield used in the artwork prompt | Source and qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hungary | Eight alternating red and silver horizontal bands, beginning red | [Hungarian constitutional description](https://ugyeszseg.hu/repository/mkudok208.pdf) confirms the banded component; [Hungarian National Library historical heraldry study](https://mek.oszk.hu/07300/07392/07392.pdf) discusses the red/silver Árpád component. We use this simplified historic component, not a claim to reproduce Vladislaus I's full royal arms. |
| Poland | White eagle on red, gold beak/claws, open gold crown, facing heraldic right (viewer left) | [Virtual Museum of the White Eagle: Jagiellonian era](https://orzelbialy.edu.pl/czesc-3/) explains medieval open crowns and the surviving Jagiełło tomb eagle; no claim that a modern reconstructed banner is a surviving original. |
| Bohemia | Silver crowned double-tailed lion on red | [Czech government: history of the national arms](https://vlada.gov.cz/cz/clenove-vlady/historie-minulych-vlad/historie-statniho-znaku/statni-znak-ceske-republiky--jeho-predchudci-a-soucasna-podoba-43755/). Keep the two tails distinct. |
| Austria | Red shield with one silver horizontal band | [Hermann Wiesflecker, The Austrian Bindenschild](https://www.historischerverein-stmk.at/wp-content/uploads/Z_SB25_Hermann-WIESFLECKER-Der-%C3%B6sterreichische-Bindenschild.pdf), published by the Historical Association for Styria. No republican eagle, hammer/sickle, or modern state accessories. |
| Styria | Silver heraldic panther on green, red mouth flame, horns and claws | [Styrian regional administration: heraldic explanation](https://www.verwaltung.steiermark.at/cms/beitrag/11681353/74837745/), and [historical regional images](https://www.verwaltung.steiermark.at/cms/ziel/74835146/DE/). A heraldic panther is not a natural spotted leopard or an ordinary lion. |
| Croatia | Three crowned gold lion/leopard heads on blue, two above one below | [Croatian Encyclopedia, medieval Croatian/Dalmatian heraldry](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/grb-hrvatski), section “Grb Kraljevine Dalmacije”, documents the shared medieval territorial sign. The previous draft's Talovac black cross was a family/campaign reference and is not used as the national shield. The checkerboard is not substituted into the 1444-oriented set. |
| Transylvania | Eagle, sun, crescent and seven towers, with gold upper/red lower fields and silver towers | [Hubert de Vries: Transylvanian heraldic history](https://www.rode-leeuw.nl/RomTransilvania1.html) reproduces seventeenth-century seals, coins and evolving arrangements. The user approved the later motifs. The artwork is a simplified seventeenth-century-inspired adaptation, not a verified independent Transylvanian coat of arms for 1444. Do not mislabel the later standardized blue/gold 1765 arrangement as seventeenth-century. |

No web image files are copied into the deliverables. The generator draws stylized motifs from these descriptions. Individual generated details still require visual review; the source check is not a claim that every generated crest is an exact heraldic reproduction.

Frames: Hungary bronze/ochre; Poland ivory/crimson; Bohemia plum/silver; Austria crimson/ivory; Styria green/brass; Croatia charcoal/ivory; Transylvania slate blue/gold. The shared back has no crest or nation identifier, so it does not reveal a hidden card's faction or type.
